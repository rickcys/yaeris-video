## Full script (verbatim, 8 lines)

1. Scraping cost estimates are usually vague marketing language, not actual math you can run yourself.
2. There are two fundamentally different ways this pricing gets described, and only one of them has a real number attached.
3. The vague version: "affordable," "cost-effective," with nothing you can actually calculate against your own volume.
4. The actual math: 2 credits, 2 cents, per crawl, and that covers everything, including proxy rotation and headless browser escalation when a page needs it.
5. The test is running your own real page volume through that number. Ten thousand pages a month comes out to roughly two hundred dollars, no separate charge for the harder pages that needed more than a simple fetch.
6. There's no separate proxy fee, no separate browser-rendering fee, and the credits never expire if volume drops one month.
7. The real comparison was never this number against a competitor's vague pricing page. It's this number against what building and maintaining that infrastructure yourself would actually cost.
8. Follow for more of this kind of content.
