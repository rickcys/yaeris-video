Easy to deploy usually just means
someone else is quietly doing the
DevOps work for you.
The question is whether that someone
is you.
There are two fundamentally different ways
to run a headless CMS,
and most people don't realize which
one they signed up for until
something breaks.
The self-hosted way,
you provision a server,
write your own deploy script,
renew your own TLS certificates,
set up your own database backups,
and get paged yourself when disk
space fills up.
None of that shows up as
a line item anywhere.
It's just time every week.
The push to deploy way.
You connect a Git repo,
and every push to your tracked
branch builds and ships itself automatically,
with zero downtime switching to the
new version.
The tell is simple.
Time how long your actual first
deploy takes, start to a live
URL. If it's an afternoon of
server configuration, that's the self-hosted path.
If it's four steps and a
few minutes, that's the other one.
This is the category Launchpad is
built in to connect your Git
repo, bring your own database,
get a live preview URL on
every branch, and roll back instantly
if something breaks.
The free personal plan alone covers
most small projects,
100 build minutes and 10 gigabytes,
no card required.
Before you call a host easy,
ask what happens the week something
actually breaks.
