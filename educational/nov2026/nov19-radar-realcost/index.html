Credit based pricing looks confusing
right up until you actually sit
down and do the math once.
There are two fundamentally different ways
people relate to this pricing, and
only one of them is based
on real numbers. The vague version,
pay as you go, with no
actual number attached, which is exactly
what makes it feel riskier than
it is.
The actual math. One credit is
a cent, an email costs one
credit, an AI call or a
crawl costs two credits, two cents
each.
The test is running your own
real monthly volume through that formula.
A thousand emails, five hundred calls,
five hundred crawls comes out to
three thousand credits, about thirty dollars.
The minimum top up is $50
for 5,000 credits, and those credits
don't expire, so a slow month
doesn't waste anything, it just carries
forward.
The scary part was never the
pricing model itself. It was never
actually running the numbers before deciding
it felt risky.
